URL; verify amendments and original versus restated definitions. Companyfacts
labels nominate evidence; original filing declarations bind fiscal identity.

Use only economic information eligible at the assessment cutoff. A later
filing's prior-year comparative row is not evidence that the row was available
at the earlier cutoff. Keep unavailable/future rows visibly quarantined. Current
endpoint caches and a few long observation spans do not establish twenty years
of original-vintage quarterly coverage. Enumerate the expected source-declared
quarters and inspect original evidence before making that coverage claim.

Market price, currency, rates, industry studies and macro inputs need their own
observation/publication dates and units. A day cutoff does not prove intraday
tradability. A 2026 reconstruction can use a genuinely 2026-authored method or
assumption while economic inputs remain limited to 2024; label that authorship
explicitly, never backdate it or classify a future economic fact as an assumption.
Historical reconstruction is not a blind forecast or a contemporaneous report.

Store a stable logical source ID across revisions of the same economic input;
give a new financial quarter its own fiscal-bound ID. Retain old revisions and
raw originals. Compare relevant projections with JSON types and units intact:
`true`, `1` and `"1"` differ. A raw hash supports provenance, not semantic
equivalence. JSON formatting/mtime alone does not change an analysis, whereas
free-text revisions cannot be dismissed as formatting without verifying their
meaning; the text-source contract retains the full original in `raw_text`.

## Original reading coverage

Maintain a compact claim-to-source/read-coverage record in the analysis. Identify
sections and passage/table references actually examined, the dimensions they
support, unread exclusions and the effect on conclusions. Downloading a complete
HTML document, extracting declarations or checking three tables is not reading
the entire filing. Selected excerpts can support a bounded question, never a
claim of complete investment diligence. Do not claim unseen sections were read.

Read original narrative alongside numbers: business and competition; MD&A's
drivers and reconciliations; financial statement notes for accounting, segments,
commitments and contingencies; risk factors; capital allocation and the relevant
governance/proxy disclosures. Select coverage according to the claim, not an
invented mandatory word-for-word reading rule. If necessary passages are absent,
acquire them when authorized or narrow the conclusion and leave required work
pending. Issuer press releases and companyfacts are useful entry evidence, not
substitutes for original passages supporting the claimed dimensions.

## Execute substantive questions

Follow the folder's configured questions and dependency graph. For each affected
question state the question, eligible evidence, reasoning, strongest competing
explanation, missing evidence and what would change the answer. Preserve valid
independent answers. Recompute dependent synthesis when evidence, assumptions,
method versions or declared question dependencies change. Do not reduce required
questions or rebind old outputs to new dependencies to create apparent completion.

Cover the applicable dimensions without duplicating prose across files:

- Business/competition: distinguish demand, volumes, price, mix, currency,
  customer concentration, distribution and competitive durability. Name which
  drivers are observed and which are hypotheses; missing segment weights prevent
  combining unlike growth percentages.
- Financial condition: reconcile income, balance sheet and cash flows; inspect
  accruals, working capital, financing, debt/liquidity and relevant note disclosures.
  Preserve reported definitions, units, duration starts, annual/YTD versus quarter
  values and reported/restated versions. Q4 acquisition selects an annual FY
  endpoint, not automatically a standalone Q4 statement. Never subtract EPS or
  average shares; only derive additive flows with compatible original periods and
  explicit reconciliation, showing the calculation and its limits.
- Capital allocation/governance: inspect investment, distributions, dilution,
  incentives, control and related-party issues where relevant. A buyback total
  alone cannot establish value creation or management alignment.
- Valuation/scenarios: name the model and why it suits the asset, retain dated
  price/share/FX inputs and distinguish operating forecasts from security returns.
  Show reproducible calculations and sensitivity to material assumptions. A next
  quarter earnings bridge cannot justify a multi-year terminal growth rate,
  valuation multiple or benchmark return without separate evidence.
- Risks/synthesis: test the preferred thesis against opposing evidence, unresolved
  drivers and alternative causal explanations. Explain changes from the predecessor
  and across quarters, including changed definitions or acquired business scope.

Refuse unsuitable model transfer. Bank funding, credit losses and regulatory
capital call for different metrics from industrial debt/enterprise-value or
CFO-minus-capex shortcuts; equity/dividend/excess-return approaches are candidates
only with adequate inputs. Energy requires volumes, realizations, cycle and
maintenance-investment distinctions; a profitable quarter is not mid-cycle
earnings and negative earnings do not yield a meaningful positive trailing P/E.
Cloud/subscription and membership retail require their own segment, renewal,
investment and unit-economic definitions. Deposit protection or a bank asset
transfer does not establish recovery for listed-parent shareholders. The
repository's `docs/finance/pilots/industry-contrasts-v1` supplies method contrasts,
not accepted full company research, calibrated models or expected excess returns.
ETF, commodity and FX judgments need their own applicable methods and evidence;
do not relabel an equity framework as verified support for those assets.

## Conditional horizons

Separate analysis windows from the user's unknown holding preference. Preserve
an established method's durations and state their anchor date and boundaries.
The initial pilot's short 0–3, medium 3–12 and long 12–60 months differs from
the later exploratory one-quarter / 1–3-year / 5+-year convention. Name the
selected convention and this difference explicitly; never silently merge the
two, use one quarter to support a five-year forecast or present either as a
user-confirmed holding period. A changed convention is an authored method
change with affected comparisons, not a cosmetic label edit. Where no window
is established, identify the proposed analysis windows and any decision-relevant
ambiguity rather than inventing the user's preference.

For each of `short`, `medium`, `long`, record:

- Explicit duration/anchor; a conditional judgment or `insufficient_evidence`.
- The relevant eligible evidence and counterevidence; distinguish business
  direction from price/return implications and identify unsupported links.
- Material assumptions and observable invalidation triggers, with the next
  evidence needed to test them.

Do not invent scenario probabilities, benchmark returns, price targets or
precision. Without dated price, suitable valuation inputs or sufficient driver
evidence, refrain from an investment rating. `insufficient_evidence` is not hold,
neutrality or expected zero return. A horizon can have useful conditional
operating reasoning while its investment conclusion remains insufficient.

## Completion and continuation

Check sources, citations, calculations and counterevidence against the actual
output before writing its verification record. Correct arithmetic and matching
hashes do not establish source coverage or substantive quality. Record author
self-checks honestly; do not label them independent review. Unresolved required
analysis remains pending even when a partial research envelope can be saved.

When sufficient evidence exists for an independent scoped unit, persist that
unit's analysis plus its real economic dependencies and local method/assumption
identities in a managed partial checkpoint, keeping blocked dimensions pending.
This lets the next invocation reuse actual progress rather than rediscovering a
standalone partial note. If an entire question still lacks necessary coverage,
retain its partial findings without declaring that question complete. Explain
that distinction explicitly. Complete publication requires all required questions
and gaps resolved; structural validation cannot make that decision for the agent.

Retain original source hashes, exact relevant projections, fiscal coverage,
cutoff, actual method/assumption authorship, horizon convention, changes,
predecessor link and unresolved gaps in the review. Keep generic methodology in
this reference; an applied-method manifest needs a content/version identity and
selected conventions, not a copied manual. Preserve all reports and user edits.
Source-cache audits, successful helpers and industry examples leave their stated
runtime and research limitations intact. Installed-host continuation, actual
external freshness, analytical sufficiency and program completion each require
their own observed evidence.
# Obsidian Finance

Optional `obsidian-finance` 0.1.0 provides `sec-data` for public SEC filing
metadata/financial observations and `finance-research` for agent-led research
continuation from a designated folder. It runs independently of the general-note
`obsidian` plugin and the original Monkey Skills project.

Claude and Codex manifests register this separate package in the repository's
local marketplace. Registration is not installation or evidence of fresh-session
skill execution. No global plugin refresh or host installation is performed;
OpenCode support is unverified and no finance loader is shipped for that host.

## Run directly

Python 3.11 or later is the only runtime requirement; all runtime imports use
the standard library. Configure `SEC_USER_AGENT` locally with the operator's
genuine identifying name and contact email before running. Do not reuse an
example identity or commit contact details. Requests send this identity and
the public issuer request to SEC; private vault content is not used.

From the repository root:

```sh
python3 obsidian-finance/skills/sec-data/scripts/acquire.py \
  --ticker AAPL \
  --fiscal-quarter 2024Q3 \
  --output-dir /private/tmp/sec-data-sample
```

`YYYYQq` selects the company's fiscal quarter. Without `--as-of`, the data
cutoff is the UTC acquisition date and `mode` is `retrospective`; add
`--as-of 2024-08-15` for an explicit `historical` data snapshot. Both summary
and manifest record `effective_cutoff` and the source-bound `resolution`.
These are data-acquisition dates, not investment-assessment dates. Market,
macro and other research dimensions may need different observation periods
and their own assessment cutoff; this operation does not acquire them.

The compatible observation-end range entry still requires all three dates:

```sh
python3 obsidian-finance/skills/sec-data/scripts/acquire.py \
  --ticker AAPL \
  --period-start 2024-01-01 \
  --period-end 2024-12-31 \
  --as-of 2024-12-31 \
  --output-dir /private/tmp/sec-data-sample
```

The explicitly requested output root may be created when its direct parent
already exists. Each invocation creates a new `sec-run-*` directory; existing
research and previous runs remain unchanged. stdout is one JSON summary;
stderr carries progress. Missing identity or invalid input returns exit 64
before any request.
Quarter and date-range selectors cannot be mixed.

## Fiscal-period evidence

Companyfacts `fy/fp` labels only nominate original 10-Q/10-K primary documents
whose accession and observation endpoint match submissions `reportDate`.
Quarter identity requires the primary document's same-context inline-XBRL
DEI declarations for fiscal year, fiscal period, period endpoint and issuer CIK.
The resolution retains original accession/filing date, literal period end,
declarations/context references and source URL/hash/retrieval time. It does not
guess a quarter start, use calendar months or today's fiscal-year end.

Supported period-end formats are ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` and the inline transformation
`date-monthname-day-year-en` (for example, `June 29, 2024`). Unsupported
transforms, missing or contradictory declarations, ambiguous originals and
unavailable eligible primary documents return `FISCAL_PERIOD_UNRESOLVED` /
exit 1, with raw diagnostics but no eligible dataset or completion marker.
Declaration context matching checks `contextRef` agreement, not full XBRL
context semantics. Primary-document basenames accept only letters, digits,
underscores and hyphens followed by `.htm` or `.html`.

Quarter mode selects observations with exactly the resolved end date while
preserving their original start dates and versions. Q4 binds an original 10-K's
`FY` endpoint; annual/YTD values remain annual/YTD, without Q4 subtraction.

## Data and status

Each finalized usable run contains:

- `manifest.json`: request, issuer, per-source URLs, retrieval timestamps,
  raw payload hashes, errors, limitations and output paths.
- `raw/tickers.json`, `raw/submissions.json`, `raw/companyfacts.json` and
  `raw/archives/`: acquired responses saved verbatim when available. Quarter
  requests also retain candidate `raw/primary-<accession>.html` documents.
- `eligible-facts.json`: observation rows with taxonomy, concept, unit, value,
  duration/instant dates, accession, filed date and source evidence.
- `filings.json`: 10-K, 10-Q and amendments published on or before cutoff,
  without a lower publication bound.
- `coverage.json`: observed spans, exclusion counts and inventory limitations.
- `completion.json`: actual status of a fully written usable run.

Required-source failures take precedence over empty results. `complete` / exit
0 means eligible facts and all required sources; `partial` / exit 2 means a
required source or archive failed; `empty` / exit 3 means no eligible facts
with all required sources valid, even if filings exist. `failed` / exit 1 means
no trustworthy issuer or usable financial endpoint section, or an output error.
Unresolved fiscal-period identity also forces failed / exit 1 before other statuses.
Source-failed runs may retain a diagnostic manifest and raw evidence but have
no eligible dataset or
completion marker. Output errors may leave unfinished files without that marker;
do not use them as completed data. Retry into a new run.

Observation end dates are filtered inclusively by the requested period; filed
dates are filtered inclusively by cutoff. Missing or invalid publication dates
are excluded; conflicting accession or acceptance-date evidence is quarantined.
Acceptance before the official filing date is valid; preserve both and use the
official filing date for cutoff eligibility. Malformed acceptance timestamps or
acceptance dates after the official filing date remain conflicts.
Unknown inventory accessions retain their filed date and reconstructable SEC
URL with an explicit inventory-absence label. Distinct reported versions remain
distinct; duration start dates preserve annual, year-to-date and shorter facts.

Raw responses are unfiltered latest endpoint evidence, not historical
snapshots. A day cutoff does not establish intraday trading availability.
Observed spans do not establish complete quarterly statements, custom segment
metrics or twenty-year analysis. The acquisition operation provides no quarterly
arithmetic, narrative analysis, investment recommendations or automatic note
updates.

## Read-only local work assessment (prototype v1)

This separate operation explains research work from explicitly supplied local
evidence. It does not acquire sources, execute research, resume an agent,
automatically recover work, or maintain notes in an installed host. External
freshness is always `unverified`; even `no_analysis_update` applies only to the
supplied evidence. It never scans a vault or parses the draft pilot
`research-state.json` directly. Adapt those records explicitly first.

```sh
python3 obsidian-finance/scripts/assess_work.py \
  --root /private/tmp/finance-assessment-example \
  --snapshot snapshot.json --mode historical \
  --cutoff 2024-05-03T23:59:59-04:00
```

All four arguments are required. `--mode` is `historical` or `latest`;
`--cutoff` is an ISO timestamp with timezone. Historical cutoff must match
every supplied saved record cutoff. Latest uses the requested cutoff without
changing saved records. stdout contains one JSON result on success; diagnostics
and recovery instructions use stderr. Exit 0 means a completed local assessment
(possibly listing unfinished research), 2 means partial/uncertain evidence,
1 means invalid/unreadable snapshot, and 64 means invalid invocation. An
interrupted process has no persisted job; retry revalidates all inputs.

The Python entry is `run(root, snapshot, mode, cutoff, *, stdout=None,
stderr=None) -> int`; streams default to the process streams. `main(argv=None)`
implements the CLI. Neither entry writes files. The pure core
`work_assessment.assess(snapshot, mode, cutoff)` consumes normalized data rather
than reading paths; the adapter supplies file content to that core.

The exact adapter input is a JSON object with integer `version: 1`, `sources`
and `records` arrays (empty arrays produce evidence gaps). Duplicate JSON keys,
duplicate IDs, non-finite numbers and invalid field types are rejected.

- Each source has nonempty `id`, `issuer`, `kind`; `version` and `available_at`
  are strings or absent/null when unknown. `available_at` is a date or an ISO
  timestamp with timezone; dates become end of day in the assessment timezone.
  Missing/unverified availability is excluded and reported. Supply relevant
  values including units in `projection`, or use `projection_ref` to a JSON
  file; these two fields cannot coexist. Optional `consumers` lists work IDs.
  Financial originals use `kind: financial_original` and source-declared
  `fiscal: {year, period, period_end, original_accession}`; `year` is an integer,
  `period` is Q1–Q4 and `period_end` is an ISO date. Missing fiscal evidence
  produces a gap rather than an inferred quarter.
- Each record has unique `id`, `issuer`, saved timezone-aware `cutoff`,
  `required_sources` (source IDs), `work` array and its saved `fiscal` identity.
  Optional `execution_state`, `last_attempt`, `last_success` remain observations;
  assessment never advances or clears them.
- Each work item has unique `id`, `status` (`complete` or `missing`), optional
  `depends_on` work IDs and `consumed` mapping from source ID to exactly
  `{version, projection}`. Completed work requires `output: {path, digest}`
  and `completion_evidence`, a path to nonempty local verification text.
  `digest` is SHA256 of the output's decoded UTF-8 text encoded as UTF-8
  (universal newline normalization applies). Output text is read and its digest
  checked; a filename or inline `content` never proves completion. Missing or
  mismatched completion evidence prevents reuse. Referenced unreadable files
  invalidate the input; absent completion fields instead produce work gaps.

Use stable logical source IDs across updates, such as `aapl-price`. Change
the source's `version`, `available_at` and relevant `projection` when evidence
changes, retaining the old identity/projection under saved `consumed`. A fresh
source ID alone does not express replacement of an old consumed dependency.
Projection comparison is structured equality; no arbitrary selectors run.
File mtime and unrelated metadata do not establish analytical change. A version
is supplied identity, not a raw-file hash or proof of external authenticity.

`--snapshot`, `projection_ref`, output `path` and `completion_evidence` are
relative to the explicit root. Absolute paths, `..`, and all symlinks (including
in-root symlinks) are rejected before reading targets. Only regular UTF-8 local
files are read. URLs, commands and note instructions remain inert data; unknown
metadata is ignored, never interpreted as a file reference or executable code.
Root-relative reads also use no-follow file descriptors to prevent a swapped
symlink from escaping the boundary.

Result v1 contains `scope: supplied_local_evidence`, `external_freshness:
unverified`, mode, cutoff, status, and stable arrays `decisions`, `gaps`,
`excluded`, `reusable`, `observations`. Decisions identify record, action, work,
evidence, reason and affected dimensions; gaps identify evidence and recovery.
Repeated unchanged inputs return the same result. No live source-discovery or
investment-completion claim follows from this result.

### Isolated Apple example

Run this from the repository root to create a new isolated sample using the
committed Apple Q2 reported evidence. This explicit adaptation retains the
source data and declares forecast work missing; it makes no claim that the
existing pilot's saved dependencies or outputs have been independently verified.
The setup writes only its new temporary directory; the assessor reads it.

```sh
python3 - <<'PY'
import json
from pathlib import Path
import tempfile

pilot = Path("docs/finance/pilots/apple-2024q2-q3/forecast-v1")
root = Path(tempfile.mkdtemp(prefix="finance-assessment-", dir="/private/tmp"))
reported = json.loads((pilot / "reported-inputs.json").read_text())
row = next(row for row in reported["fact_rows"] if row["id"] == "q2.opex")
projection = {"concept": row["concept"], "value": row["val"], "unit": row["unit"],
              "start": row["start"], "end": row["end"]}
(root / "projection.json").write_text(json.dumps(projection))
fiscal = dict(year=row["fy"], period=row["fp"], period_end=row["end"],
              original_accession=row["accn"])
source = dict(id="q2-opex", issuer="AAPL", kind="financial_original",
              version=row["accn"], available_at=row["filed"], fiscal=fiscal,
              projection_ref="projection.json", consumers=["forecast"])
record = dict(id="aapl-2024q2", issuer="AAPL", fiscal=fiscal,
              cutoff=reported["known_as_of"], required_sources=["q2-opex"],
              work=[dict(id="forecast", status="missing", depends_on=[],
                  consumed={"q2-opex": dict(version=row["accn"], projection=projection)})])
(root / "snapshot.json").write_text(json.dumps(dict(version=1, sources=[source], records=[record])))
print(root)
PY
# Use the printed directory as ROOT, then:
python3 obsidian-finance/scripts/assess_work.py \
  --root "$ROOT" --snapshot snapshot.json --mode historical \
  --cutoff 2024-05-03T23:59:59-04:00
```

Set `ROOT` to the printed path before the second command. Expected result:
exit 0 with `resume_incomplete_work` for forecast and unverified external
freshness. The example supplies one relevant financial observation, not a full
financial-analysis dataset. Existing acquisition remains the separate operation
documented above.

## Read-only saved-folder discovery (metadata v1)

`scripts/research_store.py` provides `discover(root, mode, cutoff) -> dict`.
The root is an explicitly selected local research folder containing the managed
`investing/` subtree. Mode and timezone-aware assessment cutoff are explicit;
the result keeps the accepted assessor's supplied-local-evidence scope and
unverified external freshness. Discovery does not acquire sources, write notes,
publish completion, or certify the substance of investment analysis. Existing
`assess_work.py` and `work_assessment.py` interfaces remain unchanged.

The supported layout is deliberately versioned; arbitrary older Markdown is
preserved, never interpreted or migrated. Missing configuration, unsupported
metadata, escaping references, symlinks and ambiguous source identities raise
`ValueError` with recovery context. No input files change. Configure once:

```text
investing/research.json
investing/assets/equities/{asset-id}/asset.json
investing/assets/equities/{asset-id}/data/sources/{source-revision}.json
investing/assets/equities/{asset-id}/data/analysis/{analysis-id}.json
investing/assets/equities/{asset-id}/data/{original-file}
investing/assets/equities/{asset-id}/cycles/{YYYYQn}/{cutoff}-{review}/review.json
```

Every metadata object has integer `version: 1` (booleans are rejected).
Duplicate JSON keys and non-finite numbers are invalid. Asset, question, review
and logical source IDs contain only ASCII letters, digits, `_` and `-`.
Filenames do not supply fiscal identity. References are root-relative and must
stay beneath `investing/`; all directory components and regular-file reads use
no-follow descriptors, including root ancestors. Document instructions and
unknown fields remain inert data. No selectors or document commands execute.

- `research.json`: `{version: 1, assets: ["aapl"]}` enumerates asset scope.
- `asset.json`: `{version: 1, id: "aapl", issuer: "AAPL", questions: [...]}`.
  Each required question has `id`, `required_sources` (logical source IDs) and
  optional `required_analysis` (local method/assumption IDs) and `depends_on`
  (question IDs in this asset). These durable research
  requirements are configuration, not per-invocation work statuses. Empty
  requirements and unknown question dependencies are rejected. A source ID may
  use the single literal template `{fiscal}` (for example,
  `financial-{fiscal}` becomes `financial-2024Q2` for that record); no arbitrary
  selectors execute and future quarter IDs need no manual question-list edit.
  Every question's expanded required source IDs must appear in that unit's
  frozen consumed identities. Expansion uses the saved record's fiscal quarter,
  including for prices without financial fiscal metadata. An absent source or
  omitted consumed identity invalidates the question and dependent synthesis,
  including legacy reviews without frozen requirements; independent completed
  questions remain reusable.
- Source revision JSON: `version: 1`, stable logical `id`, matching `issuer`,
  `kind`, economic `identity`, `available_at`, relevant typed `projection`,
  `raw_ref`, `raw_digest` and `raw_format` (`json` or `text`). The digest is
  SHA256 of the exact original bytes. JSON originals are strictly parsed;
  text originals require the full decoded original in `projection.raw_text`
  so unverifiable prose changes cannot be dismissed as formatting. Projection
  values retain units and JSON types, including booleans. The full original
  hash supplies provenance; semantic comparison uses identity and projection.
  Relevant-field selection is a recorded author responsibility, not proof of
  authenticity or relevance. Preserve each economic revision and its original
  file instead of replacing historical metadata. Duplicate `(id, identity)`
  pairs and tied latest publication times are rejected. The latest eligible
  publication of each logical source is selected at cutoff; future and unknown
  publication revisions remain visible as exclusions/gaps, never replacements
  for an eligible original. Excluded competing revisions use an `@` plus
  identity SHA256 suffix in result IDs. Selected source IDs are namespaced
  `{asset-id}:{source-id}`. Missing identity/projection/availability remains
  incomplete evidence under the accepted assessment contract.
- Financial-original sources additionally carry source-declared
  `fiscal: {year, period, period_end, original_accession}` as documented above.
  A stable financial source ID binds one fiscal identity; new quarters use new
  IDs, while revisions of the same quarter retain its ID. Cross-quarter reuse
  of one financial source ID is rejected. Price dates never create financial
  quarters. Shared economic studies belong among the applicable source
  dependencies; local methods and assumptions use the separate analysis-input
  contract below. A 2026-authored reconstruction method is not a 2024 economic
  publication and must never acquire a fabricated historical availability date.
- Local analysis metadata under `data/analysis/{analysis-id}.json` contains
  `version: 1`, matching `id`, `kind: method` or `assumption`, actual
  timezone-aware `authored_at`, method/assumption `identity`, typed `projection`,
  `content_ref` and SHA256 `content_digest` of the exact local file bytes.
  It must not contain `available_at`, `fiscal` or `raw_ref`; economic facts and
  prices remain economic sources, never reclassified to bypass cutoff checks.
  Method/assumption kinds are rejected in economic source metadata. Local
  inputs are not screened against the economic cutoff: their actual authorship
  remains visible in each unit's frozen identity, including a 2026 method used
  to reconstruct a 2024 judgment. This does not permit future economic data.
  A method's content file may be a compact applied-version manifest rather
  than a copied generic manual. Generic methodology stays in the skill; the
  calling research agent verifies its selected bundled method's content/version
  before discovery and refreshes this local applied-version manifest when it
  changes. The read-only helper checks only in-root files; it cannot detect or
  certify changes to an external plugin skill without that refresh. Assumptions
  and their content remain local.
- `review.json`: `version: 1`, asset-local review `id`, fiscal identity,
  timezone-aware saved `cutoff`, optional `predecessor` (review ID in the same
  asset and quarter), and `units` mapping question IDs to saved results.
  Fiscal identity must match the quarter directory. Optional `execution_state`,
  `last_attempt` and `last_success` remain unmodified observations. Per quarter,
  discovery selects the sole eligible leaf of the predecessor chain. Latest
  mode includes saved cutoffs at or before the request; historical mode requires
  an exact saved cutoff. Future children never hide eligible predecessors.
  Missing target reviews, dangling or cyclic predecessors, conflicting fiscal
  identities and competing leaves produce explicit review gaps. Every retained
  quarter is considered independently; old reports remain immutable inputs.
  An asset without saved cycles derives initial missing questions from eligible
  financial originals and their fiscal identities. Without an eligible original
  it reports the missing fiscal evidence rather than inventing a quarter.
- A unit contains `consumed: {source-id: {version, projection}}`, freezing
  actual economic identity and relevant projection, `output: {path, digest}`,
  and `verification` (a root-relative JSON reference). Local inputs separately
  use `analysis_inputs: {analysis-id: {kind, version, authored_at, projection,

## Protected local publication and recovery

The same module exposes Python APIs only; it has no publication CLI. These
operations are for an explicitly authorized managed subtree or isolated copy.

The Python entry is `run(root, snapshot, mode, cutoff, *, stdout=None,
stderr=None) -> int`; streams default to the process streams. `main(argv=None)`
implements the CLI. Neither entry writes files. The pure core
`work_assessment.assess(snapshot, mode, cutoff)` consumes normalized data rather
than reading paths; the adapter supplies file content to that core.

The exact adapter input is a JSON object with integer `version: 1`, `sources`
and `records` arrays (empty arrays produce evidence gaps). Duplicate JSON keys,
duplicate IDs, non-finite numbers and invalid field types are rejected.

- Each source has nonempty `id`, `issuer`, `kind`; `version` and `available_at`
  are strings or absent/null when unknown. `available_at` is a date or an ISO
  timestamp with timezone; dates become end of day in the assessment timezone.
  Missing/unverified availability is excluded and reported. Supply relevant
  values including units in `projection`, or use `projection_ref` to a JSON
  file; these two fields cannot coexist. Optional `consumers` lists work IDs.
  Financial originals use `kind: financial_original` and source-declared
  `fiscal: {year, period, period_end, original_accession}`; `year` is an integer,
  `period` is Q1–Q4 and `period_end` is an ISO date. Missing fiscal evidence
  produces a gap rather than an inferred quarter.
- Each record has unique `id`, `issuer`, saved timezone-aware `cutoff`,
  `required_sources` (source IDs), `work` array and its saved `fiscal` identity.
  Optional `execution_state`, `last_attempt`, `last_success` remain observations;
  assessment never advances or clears them.
- Each work item has unique `id`, `status` (`complete` or `missing`), optional
  `depends_on` work IDs and `consumed` mapping from source ID to exactly
  `{version, projection}`. Completed work requires `output: {path, digest}`
  and `completion_evidence`, a path to nonempty local verification text.
  `digest` is SHA256 of the output's decoded UTF-8 text encoded as UTF-8
  (universal newline normalization applies). Output text is read and its digest
  checked; a filename or inline `content` never proves completion. Missing or
  mismatched completion evidence prevents reuse. Referenced unreadable files
  invalidate the input; absent completion fields instead produce work gaps.

Use stable logical source IDs across updates, such as `aapl-price`. Change
the source's `version`, `available_at` and relevant `projection` when evidence
changes, retaining the old identity/projection under saved `consumed`. A fresh
source ID alone does not express replacement of an old consumed dependency.
Projection comparison is structured equality; no arbitrary selectors run.
File mtime and unrelated metadata do not establish analytical change. A version
is supplied identity, not a raw-file hash or proof of external authenticity.

`--snapshot`, `projection_ref`, output `path` and `completion_evidence` are
relative to the explicit root. Absolute paths, `..`, and all symlinks (including
in-root symlinks) are rejected before reading targets. Only regular UTF-8 local
files are read. URLs, commands and note instructions remain inert data; unknown
metadata is ignored, never interpreted as a file reference or executable code.
Root-relative reads also use no-follow file descriptors to prevent a swapped
symlink from escaping the boundary.

Result v1 contains `scope: supplied_local_evidence`, `external_freshness:
unverified`, mode, cutoff, status, and stable arrays `decisions`, `gaps`,
`excluded`, `reusable`, `observations`. Decisions identify record, action, work,
evidence, reason and affected dimensions; gaps identify evidence and recovery.
Repeated unchanged inputs return the same result. No live source-discovery or
investment-completion claim follows from this result.

def _require(value, message):
    if not value:
        raise ValueError(message)


def _id(value):
    _require(isinstance(value, str) and re.fullmatch(r"[a-zA-Z0-9_-]+", value), "ambiguous identity")
    return value


def _open(root, reference, directory=False):
    path = Path(reference)
    _require(not path.is_absolute() and path.parts and path.parts[0] == "investing" and ".." not in path.parts, "escaping reference")
    fd = os.open("/", os.O_RDONLY | os.O_DIRECTORY)
    try:
        for part in Path(root).absolute().parts[1:]:
            child = os.open(part, os.O_RDONLY | os.O_DIRECTORY | os.O_NOFOLLOW, dir_fd=fd)
            os.close(fd)
            fd = child
        for index, part in enumerate(path.parts):
            flags = os.O_RDONLY | os.O_NOFOLLOW | os.O_NONBLOCK
            if directory or index < len(path.parts) - 1:
                flags |= os.O_DIRECTORY
            child = os.open(part, flags, dir_fd=fd)
            os.close(fd)
            fd = child
        return fd
    except BaseException:
        os.close(fd)
        raise


def _bytes(root, reference):
    with os.fdopen(_open(root, reference), "rb") as stream:
        _require(stat.S_ISREG(os.fstat(stream.fileno()).st_mode), "regular local file required")
        return stream.read()


def _names(root, reference):
    fd = _open(root, reference, True)
    try:
        return sorted(os.listdir(fd))
    finally:
        os.close(fd)


def _object(root, reference):
    data = _adapter._json(_bytes(root, reference).decode("utf-8"))
    _require(isinstance(data, dict) and type(data.get("version")) is int and data["version"] == 1,
             "unsupported metadata: supply version-1 investing metadata; existing notes remain unchanged")
    return data


def _unique(items, key="id"):
    result = {}
    for item in items:
        identity = _id(item[key])
        _require(identity not in result, "duplicate identity " + identity)
        result[identity] = item
    return result


def _available(value, cutoff):
    try:
        return (datetime.combine(date.fromisoformat(value), time(23, 59, 59), cutoff.tzinfo)
                if len(value) == 10 else _adapter._core._instant(value))
    except (ValueError, TypeError, AttributeError):
        return None


def _source_versions(local, cutoff):
    groups = {}
    seen = set()
    for source in local:
        sid = _id(source["id"])
        identity = (sid, source["version"])
        _require(identity not in seen, "duplicate source revision")
        seen.add(identity)
        if source.get("kind") == "financial_original":
            _require(all(previous.get("fiscal") == source.get("fiscal") for previous in groups.get(sid, [])), "financial source identity crosses fiscal quarters")
        groups.setdefault(sid, []).append(source)
    result = []
    for sid, revisions in groups.items():
        eligible = [(stamp, source) for source in revisions
                    if (stamp := _available(source.get("available_at"), cutoff)) is not None and stamp <= cutoff]
        if eligible:
            latest = max(stamp for stamp, _ in eligible)
            selected = [s for stamp, s in eligible if stamp == latest]
            _require(len(selected) == 1, "ambiguous source publication order: " + sid)
            result.extend(selected)
        for source in revisions:
            stamp = _available(source.get("available_at"), cutoff)
            if stamp is None or stamp > cutoff:
                # Keep excluded or unverifiable revisions visible without replacing
                # the stable identity of an eligible historical original.
                if len(revisions) > 1:
                    source["id"] = sid + "@" + sha256(str(source["version"]).encode()).hexdigest()
                result.append(source)
    return result


def _required(question, quarter):
    return [_id(sid.replace("{fiscal}", quarter)) for sid in question["required_sources"]]


def _required_all(questions, asset, quarter):
    return sorted({asset + ":" + sid for q in questions.values() for sid in _required(q, quarter)})


def _analysis_valid(root, base, required, consumed, record, gaps):
    """Local analysis authorship is not economic publication eligibility."""
    fields = {"kind", "version", "authored_at", "projection", "content_ref", "content_digest"}
    _require(isinstance(consumed, dict), "analysis_inputs object")
    valid = True
    for identity in sorted(set(required) | consumed.keys()):
        _id(identity)
        frozen = consumed.get(identity)
        _require(frozen is None or isinstance(frozen, dict) and set(frozen) == fields, "frozen analysis identity")
        try:
            metadata = _object(root, base + "/data/analysis/" + identity + ".json")
            _require(metadata.get("id") == identity and metadata.get("kind") in ("method", "assumption"), "local analysis identity/kind")
            _require(not {"available_at", "fiscal", "raw_ref"} & metadata.keys(), "economic evidence cannot be a local analysis input")
            _adapter._timestamp(metadata["authored_at"])
            _require(_adapter._text(metadata.get("identity")), "local analysis version")
            current = {key: metadata[key] for key in fields - {"version"}}
            current["version"] = metadata["identity"]
            content = _bytes(root, metadata["content_ref"])
            matches = (sha256(content).hexdigest() == metadata["content_digest"]
                       and frozen is not None and _adapter._core._same_json(frozen, current))
        except FileNotFoundError:
            matches = False
        if not matches:
            valid = False
            gaps.append(dict(record=record, evidence="analysis:" + identity,
                reason="Local analysis input is missing, changed or not bound to this unit",
                recovery="Review the actual authored method/assumption and recompute dependent analysis; never backdate economic availability"))
    return valid

# Plain language in conversation

How to write chat replies a user can read on the first pass: the seven
rules, how to ask a decision question, what counts as an internal term, the
five rewrite steps for "explain it more plainly", real before-and-after
examples, eight tables for common conversation situations, and the rules
for writing a table in chat.

## Scope

This guide covers chat text written for the user, including results from
subagents that you relay to the user. It never covers files you write:
intents, specs, plans, commit messages, code comments. Those follow the
rules of the skill or repository that owns them.

Reply in the user's language and script: Traditional Chinese stays
Traditional, Simplified stays Simplified.

Deliver local documents with actual absolute paths from the active worktree or
external location, including in copyable instructions. For known local Markdown
support, use the document's project-relative path as link text (external files:
basename) and absolute targets; otherwise show full absolute paths. Enclose
targets with spaces in angle brackets: `[doc.md](</worktree name/doc.md>)`. Preserve bundled references, stored
relative paths, quoted examples and incidental filenames.

## The seven rules

### 1. Put the conclusion and its impact first

The first sentence says the conclusion and what it means for the user.
Details come after.

| Hard to read | Plain |
|---|---|
| "I checked the hook, the manifest and the tests, and it turns out the event name was wrong, so..." | "The reminder now appears on every message. The cause was a wrong event name in the hook." |

### 2. Say what a thing does in plain words

Describe what the thing does. Name an internal term, file path or rule id
only when the user needs it, in brackets after the plain words.

Plain wording changes the words, never what happened: keep every fact,
number, name and outcome, and never refer to something the original did not
say.

| Hard to read | Plain |
|---|---|
| "The attestation is stale." | "The review result no longer matches the code, so the review has to run again [attestation]." |

### 3. Say it literally

State the literal version first: who does what, what the user will see,
what changes. Do not use metaphors or analogies, and do not reach for
"like", "imagine" or "think of it as". If a comparison seems needed, the
literal sentence is still missing; write that sentence instead.

| Hard to read | Plain |
|---|---|
| "Think of the hook as a doorbell for the agent." | "Each time you send a message, the hook adds a short reminder to what the agent reads." |

A sentence with no metaphor can still be hard to read. Five sentence shapes
can make the user read the sentence twice to find who did what. Rewrite
each one, and keep every fact.

| Sentence shape | Hard to read | Plain |
|---|---|---|
| A thing does what only a person does | "`is_file()` swallows the read error and returns False." | "When the read fails, `is_file()` returns False and reports no error." |
| A thing does what only a person does | 「這支函式吞掉了讀取錯誤，回傳 False。」 | 「讀取失敗時，這支函式回傳 False，不回報錯誤。」 |
| The subject is far from its verb | "The test that the reviewer who joined last week asked for after the second failure passes." | "The test passes. The reviewer who joined last week asked for it after the second failure." |
| The subject is far from its verb | 「上週加入的審查者在第二次失敗後要求補上的那個測試通過了。」 | 「那個測試通過了。它是上週加入的審查者在第二次失敗後要求補上的。」 |
| Three or more nouns in a row | "the version sync check test failure" | "the failure of the test that checks that the versions are in sync" |
| Three or more nouns in a row | 「版號同步檢查測試失敗」 | 「檢查版號是否同步的那個測試失敗了」 |
| A noun used as a verb | "I will calendar the review." | "I will put the review on the calendar." |
| A dropped connecting word | "The file the script wrote failed the check." | "The file that the script wrote failed the check." |
| A dropped connecting word | 「測試失敗，版號沒改。」 | 「測試失敗了。版號沒有改。」 |

Add a connecting word only when you know how the two parts relate, from the
original reply or from the work you did. When you do not know, write two
separate sentences and do not guess a cause.

Why: Clark, Martin and Mahowald (2026) compared text from nine frontier and
near-frontier models with text from human writers
([arXiv 2610.29162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29162)). All nine models put
a thing as the subject of a verb that only a person does more often than
the human writers did. In conversation, most of the models also wrote
longer distances between words that depend on each other, such as a subject
and its verb, more nouns in a row, and more clauses with the connecting
word dropped. Against paraphrases with the same meaning, almost all of the
models also used more nouns as verbs. Three limits of the study matter for
this guide: it ran no human reading experiment, it measured English only,
and it did not test whether instructing a model reduces these shapes. The
Traditional Chinese pairs are this guide's own extension, not a finding of
the study.

### 4. Turn abstract nouns into what the user does and sees

Replace an abstract noun with "you do X, you see Y".

| Hard to read | Plain |
|---|---|
| "This improves observability." | "When a request fails, you see its error message in the log." |

### 5. Ask decision questions with real alternatives

A yes-or-no confirmation (publish, delete, confirm a restatement) is asked
directly, with no invented alternatives. When asking or answering how to do
something (you ask the user to decide how, or the user asks you how), the
reply lists at least two workable alternatives and marks the one you
recommend. Before sending, check three commonly missed alternatives: do
nothing or later, a smaller or simpler version, and combining two of the
listed options. For each of the three, the reply either lists it or says in
one short clause why it is ruled out, however many other options are listed;
when only two alternatives remain, those clauses say why there is no third.
Give each option one sentence on what choosing it changes for the user.

Why: in Paul Nutt's study of 400 business decisions, about half failed, and
failure was more likely when the first idea was adopted
([Ohio State News](https://news.osu.edu/half-of-business-decisions-fail-because-of-managements-blunders-new-study-finds/)).
In Nutt's 1999 study, managers developed multiple options in fewer than 20
percent of their decisions; when they did, success rates rose from 56 percent
to 70 percent
([Academy of Management Executive 13(4)](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/AME.1999.2570556)).
Chernev et al. (2015) found that choice overload depends on the context
rather than on the number of options alone
([Journal of Consumer Psychology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057740814000916)),
so list the workable options and leave out filler.

Hard to read: "Should I refactor the parser?"

Plain: one line of question, then a Decision consequences table
(situation 1 below) with the recommended option marked.

"How should the parser change?"

| Option | What you gain | What you give up | Best if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fix only the failing case (recommended) | One file changes today | Other cases stay as they are | The bug blocks a release |
| Rewrite the parser | Every input format gets retested | Two days of work | More formats are coming |
| Leave it for now | No work now | The error stays until next week | Nobody hits it yet |

Combining the fix and the rewrite is ruled out: the rewrite already covers the
failing case.

| Hard to read | Plain |
|---|---|
| "Do you want option A, B or C for publishing?" (for a publish confirmation) | "Publish the page now?" |

### 6. Show shaped content as tables or diagrams

Option comparisons, flows of three or more steps, branches and state
changes go into a table or diagram, following loom-visualization's
`SKILL.md`.

| Hard to read | Plain |
|---|---|
| Three paragraphs, each describing one option's cost, speed and risk | One table with a row per option and columns for cost, speed and risk |

### 7. Give only what the user needs

Leave out process detail unless the user asks for it.

Being concise means leaving out content that the user does not need. It
never means dropping the words that connect a sentence: keep "that",
"because", "so", 「因為」 and 「所以」 where the original has them or the
relation is known.

| Hard to read | Plain |
|---|---|
| "First I grepped the repo, then I read four files, then I ran the tests twice..." | "The tests pass. Ask if you want the steps I took." |

## Internal terms

Replace these with what they do before sending, or add them in brackets
after the plain words when the user needs the name:

- loom station names (capture-intent, write-spec, write-plan, build,
  review, ship, maintain)
- gate ids and rule ids
- requirement ids and finding ids (REQ-3, F-4)
- lane
- attestation
- probe
- temporary names the agent coined during the work
- file names and function names

## Rewrite steps

When the user asks for a plainer explanation, rewrite the last reply in
this order:

1. Make the first sentence the conclusion: the most important news or the answer. Put no announcement before it ("let me explain again", 「我重新說明一次」), no heading, and no background.
2. Replace every internal term with what it does, and turn each abstraction into who does what and what the user sees.
3. Keep the facts: rewriting changes wording, never what happened; keep every fact, number, name and outcome from the original, and keep its lead news. Translate a term by saying what it does instead of substituting a different thing: a review stays a review, and an agent is not a person; never refer to something the original did not say.
4. Cut process detail the user's decision does not need; never cut the lead news or an outcome.
5. Check for metaphor words ("like", "imagine", "think of it as", analogies) and for the five sentence shapes of rule 3 before sending; replace each metaphor with the literal statement and rewrite each shape as rule 3 shows.

## Conversation situation tables

Eight situations that come up in chat replies. Each table below is an
example: keep the headers, replace the rows, keep cells short.

### 1. Decision consequences

When to use: the user must choose how to do something, and each choice
changes what they get. This differs from `templates/01-option-comparison.md`:
that template scores options on measured criteria (latency, cost); this
table shows what each choice gains and gives up for the user.

| Option | What you gain | What you give up | Best if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fix the one case (recommended) | Done today | Other cases stay | The bug blocks a release |
| Rewrite the parser | All formats handled | Two days of work | More formats are coming |
| Wait | No work now | Error stays a week | Nobody hits it yet |

### 2. Before and after

When to use: something changed, such as behaviour, wording or a setting,
and the user needs to see what differs.

| Item | Before | After | Impact on you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reminder timing | Once per session | Every message | Rules stay in effect |
| Error text | "E42" | "File not found" | You see the cause |
| Default depth | Full check | Short check | Faster small changes |

### 3. Progress report

When to use: several pieces of work are under way and the user wants to know
where each one stands.

| Item | Status | Blocked on | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Login page | Done | None | None |
| Export | In progress | None | Add CSV test |
| Billing | Blocked | API key | You send the key |

### 4. Acceptance checklist

When to use: checking whether a change meets its criteria, or is ready to
ship.

| Criterion | Met? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tests pass | Yes | 42 passed |
| Works offline | No | Cache missing |
| Docs updated | Not applicable | No user-facing change |

### 5. Confirmed, unconfirmed, to decide

When to use: some facts are verified, some are assumed, and some need the
user's decision.

| Item | Status | Basis or next step |
|---|---|---|
| Crash cause | Confirmed | Reproduced in a test |
| Affects Windows | Unconfirmed | Run on Windows CI |
| Support old format | To decide | You choose yes or no |

### 6. Findings and recommendations

When to use: a review, audit or investigation found problems and each needs
a recommended fix.

| Problem | Severity | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Password in log | High | Remove the log line |
| Slow search | Medium | Add an index |
| Typo in button | Low | Fix the text |

### 7. Risks

When to use: a plan could go wrong in known ways and the user needs to weigh
them.

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upgrade needs re-approval | High | Feature stays off | Say so in release notes |
| Migration loses data | Low | Records gone | Back up first |
| Slower first load | Medium | Users wait longer | Load in background |

### 8. Support matrix

When to use: a feature works in some environments and not others.

| Feature | Environment A | Environment B | Environment C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reminder | Yes | Yes | No |
| Mermaid display | No | Yes | Not applicable |
| Diagram check | Yes | Yes | Yes |

In a real reply, replace "Environment A / B / C" with the environment names.

## Table-writing rules and common mistakes

### Rules

1. Use a table only when each item has three or more attributes; otherwise use a list (Google, Microsoft). Exception: a key-value summary table, one label plus one value per row (such as the incident report summary S19 or the component state matrix D7), may have two columns.
2. Put the value that identifies the row in the leftmost column (Microsoft).
3. Keep columns few and cells to one line; in chat, use at most 4 to 5 columns. The one-line cell is Microsoft's rule; the column cap is derived from fitting chat width, not a published rule.
4. Write specific headers, and do not let a header and its cell form a sentence (Microsoft).
5. Order rows logically; order columns by importance, with related columns next to each other (Google, NN/g).
6. Never leave a cell blank; write None or Not applicable (Microsoft).
7. Split a large table into smaller tables (Google).
8. State the conclusion in a sentence before the table, not inside a cell.
9. Keep cells short; put explanations outside the table.
10. Use a fixed small vocabulary in status columns, such as Done, In progress, Blocked.

Rules 8 to 10 come from analysing real user complaints about hard-to-read
agent replies, not from a published source.

### Status symbols

- Distinguish status by shape, not colour alone: ✓ and ✗ read without
  colour (Microsoft adds a check mark to green and a capital X to red).
- Coloured dots (🟢🟡🔴) need a text legend, because colour must not be the
  only way information is shown (WCAG 1.4.1).
- For graded comparisons, the Japanese four-level scale ◎ ○ △ × carries more
  than a yes/no pair and does not depend on colour.

### In-cell visuals

What can go inside a table cell, and what cannot:

- Block bars (`████░░░░`): usable only with the number in the same cell. Google
  Cloud SDK's accessibility mode replaces Unicode bars with plain percentages,
  so the vendor treats a bar alone as inaccessible.
- Text sparklines (`▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█`): usable. Tufte says a sparkline can go wherever
  a word or number can, tables included. The ceiling is 8 levels.
- Check and cross marks: shape over hue; see Status symbols above.
- Coloured dots: only with a text legend (WCAG 1.4.1); see Status symbols
  above. The ◎ ○ △ × scale is there too.
- A blank cell for "no": not allowed; see table rule 6.
- Cell background colour or a heatmap: not possible in plain Markdown. GFM
  cells hold only inline content, and GitHub strips inline style and class.
- shields.io badges: usable but risky. Badges are cached by design, and a dead
  third-party badge host breaks every badge that uses it.
- `<progress>` and `<meter>`: depend on the renderer (Obsidian shows them,
  GitHub strips them). They are not interchangeable: the WHATWG specification
  says a meter should not be used to show progress.
- Inline `<svg>`: do not use; GitHub's Markdown renderer skips `<svg>`
  elements.

Layout: when cells hold text, compare items across a row; when cells hold
numbers, compare down a column so the digits line up (a Japanese table
convention).

### Table or chart

- Per item: one data point → a list; two related data points → term and
  value lines; three or more → a table (Google).
- When the user looks up or compares individual values, or the values must be
  precise, use a table. When the message is in the shape of the data (a
  trend, a pattern, an exception), use a chart. When both matter, show both
  (Stephen Few).
- A 2×2 with categorical axes (important or not × urgent or not) can be a
  table. A 2×2 with continuous axes (market share × growth rate) cannot: the
  meaning is in each item's position, so use a chart.
- When the reader compares the data in two directions, across rows and down
  columns, a table beats a chart (Datawrapper).
- A table is the formal text alternative for a complex diagram such as a
  flowchart or an org chart: W3C's own long-description example is a table
  (W3C WAI, complex images).

### Time in tables

Time goes into a table in two opposite ways:

- Time as the column axis (Q1 to Q4, measurement dates, lifecycle stages):
  replaces a drawn Gantt chart or roadmap.
- Time as a cell value (What | Who | When): replaces a checklist.

A Now-Next-Later roadmap orders its columns by confidence, not by dates
(ProdPad, a vendor source); see entry B22 in `references/tables-business.md`.
For events placed on dates, use `templates/10-timeline.md`.

### When not to use a table

- For layout: placing content side by side is not a table (GOV.UK).
- For a single column: use a list (Google, Microsoft).
- With merged cells or multi-level headers: split into several simple tables
  (Red Hat, W3C).
- With a table inside a cell: screen readers read the inner table before the
  next outer cell (WCAG F49).
- For an incomplete comparison: if an attribute is unknown for some items,
  leave that attribute out of the table (NN/g).
- In the middle of numbered steps (Google).

### Options as columns or rows

NN/g puts options in columns and attributes in rows for comparison tables.
In a narrow chat window, use columns only for three or fewer options, and
rows for more. This is a judgment for chat width, not a published rule, and
it departs from NN/g when there are more than three options.
